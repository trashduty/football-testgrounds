## Our Take

The Auburn Tigers visit the Georgia Bulldogs at Sanford Stadium. We make **Georgia -20**, compared with a market line of -19.5.

The best number we found is Georgia -19.5 at -105. We see a -1.6% edge there, but that does not clear our 3% threshold, so we are passing.

For us, Georgia’s clearest advantage is its ability to keep Auburn from turning drives into points. Our matchup numbers rate Georgia among the strongest teams at preventing scoring opportunities, and the defense has allowed just 13.4 points and 57.4 rushing yards per game without surrendering a run longer than 19 yards. Byrum Brown’s mobility gives Auburn another way to move the ball—he rushed for 237 yards through four games—but the passing matchup is much less favorable against Georgia’s defense.

The counterweight is Auburn’s ability to disrupt possessions and limit scoring opportunities. Auburn produced 29 tackles for loss and 11 sacks through four games while intercepting a pass in every game. That matters against a large favorite because negative plays and empty possessions can prevent separation. Georgia still has an efficient answer in Gunner Stockton, who entered with 13 touchdown passes against one interception, and an offense that had scored touchdowns on 24 of 28 red-zone trips. We see reasons for Georgia to control the game, but our slight difference with the market is not meaningful enough at the available price, so we remain comfortable passing.

### The Matchup To Watch

We are watching Georgia’s offensive line against Auburn’s pressure players. Georgia used the same starting five in each of its first five games and allowed only five sacks, while Auburn linebackers Xavier Atkins and Elijah Melendez combined for 13 tackles for loss and five sacks through four games.

If Georgia keeps Stockton clean, its efficiency can turn sustained drives into separation. If Auburn creates early-down losses and forces longer passing situations, it has a credible path to shorten possessions and keep the margin near the market’s expectation. That trench battle is the most important reason we do not see enough separation between our view and the current price.
