## Our Take

The Florida State Seminoles visit the Miami Hurricanes at Hard Rock Stadium. We make **Florida State +18.5**, compared with a market line of +21.

The best number we found is Florida State +20.5 at +100. We give Florida State a 54.3% chance to cover, which creates an 4.3% edge for us. That clears our 3% threshold, so Florida State is a bet.

Miami’s early production explains why the market is comfortable pricing a wide gap. Darian Mensah threw 14 touchdown passes without an interception through four games, while Miami allowed only 9.0 points per game over that stretch. We are not dismissing that dominance. Our concern is that the price asks Miami to convert it into another substantial margin, and our longer-run numbers do not separate these teams quite that aggressively.

For us, the case for Florida State is less about finding a clear matchup advantage and more about how much of Miami’s performance is already reflected in the market. Miami replaced four starting offensive linemen from 2025, although the new starting five remained intact through the first four games. There is also some backfield uncertainty: Mark Fletcher Jr. and CharMar Brown missed the Central Michigan game, and the guide provided no timetable for either return. The meaningful concern is Florida State’s defense against an offense capable of attacking both through the air and on the ground, but we think the market has stretched that concern far enough to support our position.

### The Matchup To Watch

We are watching Miami’s rushing offense against Florida State’s rush defense. Our matchup numbers identify this as Miami’s clearest advantage, and Miami’s early defensive dominance could give its offense repeated chances to lean on that edge. If Miami runs efficiently without needing Mensah to carry the offense, sustained separation becomes much more plausible. Florida State’s ability to limit that rushing efficiency is therefore central to our case.
