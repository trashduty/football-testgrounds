## Our Take

The Notre Dame Fighting Irish visit the BYU Cougars at LaVell Edwards Stadium. We make **BYU +9**, compared with a market line of +10.

The best number we found is BYU +9.5 at +100. We give BYU a 53.5% chance to cover, which creates an 3.5% edge for us. That clears our 3% threshold, so BYU is a bet.

For us, the case for BYU starts with an offense that has more than one way to sustain drives. BYU averaged 262.7 rushing yards through three games and topped 300 yards twice, while allowing only two sacks over that span. We are not treating that early production as proof against a much stronger opponent, but the combination of rushing efficiency and protection gives BYU a plausible path to avoid the kind of negative plays that create separation.

The meaningful concern is Notre Dame's defense. Our metrics rate its pass defense first in pass EPA and place it among the best units at preventing scoring opportunities. That matters because our longer-run numbers are less impressed with BYU's passing offense than its early results might suggest, even though Bear Bachmeier opened the season with 642 passing yards, six touchdowns and one interception through three games.

Notre Dame's fourth-ranked passing offense also presents a real challenge for BYU's pass defense. Still, BYU intercepted five passes in its first three games, and we see enough resistance on both sides of the ball to question whether Notre Dame should be priced to create this much separation. Our wager is less about declaring BYU the better team and more about taking the points at a number our model considers too high.

### The Matchup To Watch

We are watching BYU's passing game against Notre Dame's pass defense. Notre Dame owns the clearest matchup advantage in our numbers, so BYU cannot depend on routine dropback success. The more interesting part is whether BYU's rushing attack can keep Bachmeier out of predictable passing situations and preserve manageable downs.

If BYU can do that, its early pass protection gives the offense a chance to stay functional even without consistently winning through the air. If Notre Dame forces obvious passing downs, its defensive advantage becomes much more likely to control the game.
