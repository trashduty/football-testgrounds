## Our Take

The Washington State Cougars visit the Oregon State Beavers at Reser Stadium. We make **Washington State +13.5**, compared with a market line of +17.5.

The best number we found is Washington State +18.5 at -110. We give Washington State a 60.2% chance to cover, which creates an 7.9% edge for us. That clears our 3% threshold, so Washington State is a bet.

We understand why the market is willing to price Oregon State aggressively. Braden Atkinson has surpassed 300 passing yards in all four games, while Jesse Legree has opened the season with four consecutive 100-yard receiving performances. We are not dismissing that explosiveness, but our numbers distinguish between piling up passing yards and consistently creating scoring opportunities. That is where Washington State’s defense matches up better than the headline production suggests. Oregon State has converted every red-zone trip, but our view is that getting into scoring position often enough remains the more important question.

The concern for us is Washington State’s offense. It has converted only 28.9 percent of its third downs, and its inefficient passing game now faces an Oregon State defense averaging 3.5 sacks per game. That creates real risk of stalled possessions and poor field position. Still, Caden Pinnick offers an important counter with his mobility: through four games, he had 201 rushing yards and 13 runs of at least 10 yards.

For us, the case is less about Washington State matching Oregon State’s passing production and more about whether Oregon State can turn its yardage advantage into enough separation. Our model is skeptical because Washington State’s defensive profile is stronger in the areas that limit scoring opportunities, while Oregon State’s rushing efficiency has not provided the same consistent support as its passing game.

We are watching Washington State’s pass protection against Oregon State’s pressure. Washington State allowed six sacks against Arizona, and Oregon State recorded eight against UTEP. If Oregon State repeatedly creates negative plays, Washington State’s offensive limitations become much harder to overcome.

Pinnick’s ability to escape pressure matters here. We do not need Washington State to win the passing matchup outright, but we do need it to avoid giving Oregon State extra possessions and short fields. If Pinnick can turn some pressure into positive runs, Washington State has a better path to sustaining enough offense for our position.
