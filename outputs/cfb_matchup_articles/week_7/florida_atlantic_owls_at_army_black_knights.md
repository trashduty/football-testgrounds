## Our Take

The Florida Atlantic Owls visit the Army Black Knights at Michie Stadium. We make **Army -4**, compared with a market line of -2.5.

The best number we found is Army -2.5 at -112. We see a -0.1% edge there, but that does not clear our 3% threshold, so we are passing.

Our model’s preference for Army starts with the ground game. Our efficiency numbers grade Army’s rushing offense as a major advantage against Florida Atlantic’s run defense. The early production supports that view: Army averaged 394 rushing yards through two games, all five first-listed offensive linemen were seniors, and the offense allowed only 0.5 sacks and 3.0 tackles for loss per game. For us, that combination points to a credible path for Army to control the game rather than relying on one recent result.

There is also enough of a passing constraint to keep Florida Atlantic from selling out completely against the run. Cale Hellums completed 9 of 10 passes for 91 yards and two touchdowns against South Florida. We are not treating that performance as a new baseline, but it matters when paired with Army’s rushing efficiency because it gives the offense another way to punish an overly aggressive defense.

The concern is on the other side. Our numbers rate Florida Atlantic well in both passing efficiency and creating scoring opportunities, while Army grades poorly at preventing those opportunities. That gives Florida Atlantic a realistic route to stay within the margin even if Army moves the ball consistently. We still make Army the stronger side, but the current price does not provide enough value for us to turn that disagreement into a wager.

### The Matchup To Watch

We are watching Army’s rushing offense against Florida Atlantic’s run defense. Army had 25 rushing plays of at least 10 yards through three weeks, so this is not only an offense built around steady gains; it has also generated chunk plays. If Army continues creating those gains, it can dictate possession and limit Florida Atlantic’s chances to attack the weaker areas of Army’s defense.

That matchup explains our lean, but it also explains the pass. We need Army’s rushing advantage to translate into enough separation, not merely sustained drives. Florida Atlantic’s offensive profile leaves less room for error than the run-defense mismatch alone might suggest.
