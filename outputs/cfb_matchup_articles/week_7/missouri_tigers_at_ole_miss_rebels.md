## Our Take

The Missouri Tigers visit the Ole Miss Rebels at Vaught-Hemingway Stadium. We make **Ole Miss -7**, compared with a market line of -6.

The best number we found is Ole Miss -6.5 at -102. We see a -1.1% edge there, but that does not clear our 3% threshold, so we are passing.

Our lean toward Ole Miss starts with the passing game. Through three games, Trinidad Chambliss had thrown for 924 yards and seven touchdowns, while Ole Miss was averaging an SEC-best 330.7 passing yards and operating at one of the fastest tempos in the country. That profile matters against a Missouri defense that returned only two listed starters and lost 10 from last season’s group. We see more potential for Ole Miss to create separation through sustained passing volume than the market appears to be pricing.

The counterargument is Missouri’s rushing game. Missouri averaged 228.3 rushing yards per game last season, while Ole Miss allowed 159.0 per game through its first three contests. Our matchup numbers also identify the ground game as Missouri’s clearest offensive opening, even though they are less favorable toward its passing efficiency. At the same time, we rate Ole Miss more favorably at preventing scoring opportunities than its raw yardage totals suggest.

For us, those competing signals explain the pass. Our overall numbers prefer Ole Miss, but Missouri has a credible way to shorten the game and limit margin through the run. The market is close enough to our expectation that the available price does not leave enough room for that matchup risk.
