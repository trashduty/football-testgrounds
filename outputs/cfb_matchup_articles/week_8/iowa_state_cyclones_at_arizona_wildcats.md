## Our Take

The Iowa State Cyclones visit the Arizona Wildcats at Arizona Stadium. We make **Iowa State +9.5**, compared with a market line of +10.5.

The best number we found is Iowa State +10.5 at -112. We see a 1.9% edge there, but that does not clear our 3% threshold, so we are passing.

Our case for Iowa State staying competitive starts with a ground game that can reduce possessions and keep the offense out of obvious passing situations. Through four games, Iowa State averaged 180.5 rushing yards compared with 164.8 passing yards, while Aiden Flora produced 382 rushing yards at 7.5 yards per carry. We see that as a more credible foundation than asking the passing game to consistently attack Arizona’s biggest defensive strength.

The concern for us is drive sustainability. Iowa State converted only 31.9 percent of its third downs through four games, even though it scored on all 12 red-zone possessions. Our matchup numbers also indicate difficulty creating scoring opportunities against this Arizona defense. That combination leaves limited room for inefficient early downs or empty possessions.

We also see enough from Iowa State’s defense to understand why our expectation is slightly more favorable than the market’s. The unit allowed 272.5 yards and 16.0 points per game through four contests, with opponents converting just 28.3 percent of third downs. Still, Utah gained 411 yards, including 205 on the ground, in the most recent game. We are not treating either sample as definitive. The broader matchup supports Iowa State being competitive, but the disagreement is modest and the current price does not give us enough value to move beyond a pass.

### The Matchup To Watch

For us, the matchup to watch is Iowa State’s passing offense against Arizona’s pass defense. Our efficiency numbers identify that as Arizona’s clearest advantage, making Iowa State’s ability to stay ahead of the chains especially important. If the ground game cannot keep the offense in manageable situations, Jaylen Raynor will face a difficult assignment against the stronger part of Arizona’s defense.

Raynor completed 21 of 40 passes with one touchdown and two interceptions against Utah. We are not using one performance as a forecast, but it illustrates the risk: Iowa State’s path becomes much narrower if Arizona can force the game onto Raynor’s arm rather than allowing Flora and the rushing attack to control the offensive structure.
