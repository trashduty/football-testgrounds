## Our Take

The TCU Horned Frogs visit the Baylor Bears at McLane Stadium. We make **TCU +4**, compared with a market line of +5.5.

The best number we found is TCU +5.5 at -105. We see a 2.7% edge there, but that does not clear our 3% threshold, so we are passing.

We are not reacting to TCU’s loss at UCF or Baylor’s win over Colorado as if either result settles this matchup. Our numbers give TCU more credit because its defense allowed only 264.8 yards per game through four games and held opponents to a 24.5 percent conversion rate on third down. That matters against a Baylor passing offense whose raw yardage was productive but whose efficiency profile has been less convincing.

The concern for us is how TCU sustains offense. Its rushing efficiency runs directly into the stronger part of Baylor’s defense, potentially putting more of the game on Jaden Craig. Craig produced three 300-yard passing games in his first four starts, but his two interceptions at UCF show the downside when volume does not become points.

That leaves us with a real but modest disagreement with the market. We can make a football case for TCU staying competitive, especially if its defense controls third down, but the difficult rushing matchup and reliance on Craig create enough volatility that the available price does not justify a wager.

### The Matchup To Watch

For us, the matchup to watch is TCU’s pass protection against Baylor’s pressure. Baylor averaged 3.75 sacks and 9.0 tackles for loss through four games, while TCU had already allowed nine sacks. Craig has spread the ball around—completing passes to at least six receivers in every game—but he needs enough time for that distribution to matter.

If Baylor consistently forces long-yardage situations, TCU’s ability to create scoring opportunities may not translate into finished drives. If TCU protects Craig, we think its passing game has enough variety to explain why our view is more favorable to the road team than the market’s.
